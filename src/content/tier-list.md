# Fallen Lineage Class Tier List Breakdown

This tier list evaluates the performance, dungeon clearing efficiency, and boss viability across all available character archetypes in Fallen Lineage. 

## S/A/B Tier Classification Matrix

Our tier evaluation is strictly based on sustained damage per round (DPR), survivability during roguelite dungeon crawls, resource economy, and team utility.

| Tier | Category / Playstyle | Defining Characteristics | Target Playstyle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S-Tier** | High-Impact Anchors | Unmatched wave-clear capability, exceptional self-sustain or defensive cooldowns, minimal reliance on rare external buffs. | Solo Dungeon Crawlers & Hardcore Boss Raids |
| **A-Tier** | Reliable Specialists | Strong single-target output or indispensable crowd control; requires deliberate positioning and basic team synergy to match S-tier output. | Flexible Team Comps & Standard Progression |
| **B-Tier** | High-Investment Archetypes | High skill floor, steep stat requirements, or narrower matchup spreads; capable in skilled hands but slower to snowball early runs. | Niche Builds & Experimental Roguelite Setups |

## Why Each Tier is Positioned Here

### S-Tier Archetypes
Classes in the S tier offer consistent turns without falling prey to random critical spikes from elite mobs. They feature built-in action point recovery or multi-target cleaves that make early floor clear times twice as fast as other options.

### A-Tier Archetypes
A-tier classes have exceptional scaling once their core skill trees or passive abilities are unlocked. Their slight drawback is vulnerability in the first three dungeon rooms before primary buffs are stacked.

### B-Tier Archetypes
B-tier classes are by no means unplayable. However, in turn-based roguelite mechanics where every round counts, they often demand specific item drops or precise turn order manipulation to stay competitive with higher-tier options.
